THE BIG IDEAHOW TO READ THE SIMULATION

The evidence changed the model.

01 / MOST

Pass straight through

Most alpha particles miss the tiny, dense nucleus. That suggested atoms are mostly empty space.

02 / SOME

Veer off course

Positive alpha particles are repelled by the positive nucleus. A closer pass means a larger deflection.

03 / RARE

Turn back

A few particles scatter through very large angles. Rutherford inferred that positive charge is concentrated in a compact nucleus.

Model limits The composition changes follow nuclear bookkeeping exactly. The energy gate uses a liquid-drop approximation and can miss shell structure, excited states, and measured atomic-mass effects; it cannot predict half-lives or branching ratios. An event animation is a conservation diagram, not a claim that the selected decay is observed. The 3D directions assume an unpolarized source; beta–neutrino angular correlations are omitted. The beta spectrum uses an allowed-shape, point-Coulomb approximation, not a measured spectrum. Recoil uses relativistic kinematics and approximate nuclear masses (A × u). Nucleus sizes, recoil displacement and emission time are enlarged for visibility. The β⁺ view follows only the positron branch; electron capture, annihilation, internal conversion and subsequent daughter decays are not simulated. The population clock follows parent survival, not branching fractions or photon yields. For decay kinematics, see the Geant4 physics reference. For evaluated isotope data, see the NNDC guides to alpha decay, beta decay, and NuDat. The Tc-99m 140.5 keV preset comes from evaluated ENSDF gamma data.
